    2.2Feed

    • Main feed: gas oil, 316–566 °C (600–1050 °F); aromatic + naphthenic + paraffinic. Bp. 200
    • Primary: AGO, VGO. Lp. 20 Others: atmospheric, coker, visbreaker distillates; desulphurised AR; desulphurised + deasphalted VR. Bp. 201
    • Contaminants (S, N, metals) concentrate in heavier fractions → feed normally hydrotreated. Bp. 201 Lp. 20
    • Economics: residual fuel is worth far less than cracking feed → refiners blend AR/VR bottoms into FCC feed. Lp. 20

    2.3Catalyst

    • Must be acidic: supplies a proton (Brønsted); Lewis acids (BF₃, AlCl₃, FeCl₃) also form carbenium ions. Lpp. 10, 12
    • Cracking needs the strongest acid sites of all acid reactions (Fig 2.1). LFig 4.7
    • Generations: ① acid-treated natural aluminosilicates (clays) → ② amorphous synthetic silica–alumina → ③ crystalline zeolites (molecular sieves: more active sites + adsorption → faster cracking). Lp. 12

    Zeolite Y (active component)

    • Faujasite = natural; zeolite Y = synthetic form, the usual FCC catalyst. Lp. 13
    • Sodium form: Lp. 13
      \[ (0.9 \pm 0.2)\,\mathrm{Na_2O}\cdot \mathrm{Al_2O_3}\cdot w\,\mathrm{SiO_2}\cdot x\,\mathrm{H_2O}, \qquad 3 < w < 6,\;\; x < 9 \]
    • Na poisons cracking → ion-exchange with rare-earth cations → acid form. Lp. 13 (Slide says "cesium, lanthanum"; caesium is an alkali metal, not a rare earth — cerium-type lanthanides are meant.)
    • ~8 Å supercage admits C₁₈–C₂₅ 1-, 2-, 3-ring aromatics from VGO; powder 75 µm, ~800 m²/g. Bp. 208

    Matrix B§8.7.2, p. 209

    • Binder: "glue" giving cohesion. Filler (kaolin): body; with binder → density + attrition resistance.
    • Additives: ppm metal → CO → CO₂ in regenerator; metal oxides fix SOₓ (released as H₂S in reactor).

    2.4Quick check

    Why and how is Na removed from zeolite Y?
    Na blocks acid sites (poisons cracking); ion exchange with rare-earth cations gives the acid form. Lp. 13
    Which metal kills activity; which makes coke + H₂?
    V kills activity; Ni → coke + H₂ (passivate with Sb). Lp. 20
    Why does ZSM-5 raise octane?
    Only linear paraffins enter and crack to C₃ → remaining gasoline richer in branched paraffins + aromatics. Bp. 209
    Why low pressure (1–5 bar)?
    Cracking greatly increases moles; low P favours it. Bp. 208
