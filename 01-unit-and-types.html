    Chapter 01 · the unit and its types

    What the FCC unit is

    Definition, why a refinery needs it, how it differs from thermal cracking, and the types of catalytic cracking from Houdry's fixed beds to today's riser units.

    Sources: Lpp. 2–26 B§8.1–8.2, 8.8, 8.16Figures: 1.1–1.3

    1.1What catalytic cracking is

    • Cracking breaks large, high-boiling hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, lighter ones. With heat alone it is thermal cracking (pyrolysis); it can also be done with a catalyst. Lp. 2
    • Catalytic cracking uses an acidic catalyst. It differs from thermal cracking in mechanism: a carbenium-ion chain instead of free radicals. Lp. 9
    • It is the most widely used refinery process for turning heavy oil into high-octane gasoline. Lp. 9
    • FCC (fluid catalytic cracking) uses a fine catalyst powder (average ~70 µm) that behaves like a fluid when aerated. It circulates continuously between the reactor and the regenerator and carries heat from one to the other. Lp. 16
    • The FCC is called "the heart of the refinery": it upgrades low-value vacuum gas oil (VGO) into gasoline and C₃/C₄ olefins, which feed alkylation. Bp. 199
    • Cracking is endothermic. Burning the coke off the catalyst in the regenerator supplies the heat. Bp. 199
    One-line definition (exam)

    FCC is a continuous process in which preheated heavy gas oil is cracked over a circulating, fluidised zeolite catalyst in a riser reactor at 480–550 °C and 1–5 bar for a few seconds. It makes high-octane gasoline, LPG olefins and cycle oils. Coke on the spent catalyst is burned off in a regenerator, which supplies the heat of reaction. Bpp. 199, 208, 210

    Key point

    The process flows of all catalytic crackers are similar: hot oil meets catalyst, coke builds up and deactivates it, catalyst and vapour are separated, adsorbed oil is steam-stripped, and coke is burned off with air. Lpp. 15–16

    1.5FCC configurations

    Side-by-side book PFD type

    • Reactor and regenerator are separate vessels next to each other. Bp. 209
    • Catalyst moves through standpipes and slide valves.
    • Book Fig 8.7 PFD (chapter 3) is this type.

    Stacked (Orthoflow)

    • The reactor is mounted on top of the regenerator. Bp. 210
    • Kellogg Orthoflow design. Lp. 14
    • Kellogg units use plug valves to limit erosion from catalyst-laden streams. Lpp. 19–20

    Where the cracking happens: dense bed vs riser

    1. Until about 1965 most units cracked in a dense fluidised bed inside the reactor vessel. Severity was controlled by bed depth (time) and temperature. Lp. 16
    2. Cracking already happened in the riser (the catalyst was hottest and most active there), but riser conditions were not deliberately controlled. Lp. 16
    3. Zeolite catalysts are far more active, so much more cracking took place in the riser. Units were redesigned with a catalyst–oil separator in place of the dense-bed reactor: short contact time prevents over-cracking of gasoline to gas and coke. Lpp. 16, 19
    4. Newer designs take up to 25 % reduced crude in the feed and are built for both complete CO₂ combustion and CO-combustion control. Lpp. 19–20

    1.7Quick check

    Why can an FCC run continuously when the Houdry unit could not?
    The catalyst is a fine powder that flows like a fluid, so it circulates continuously: coked catalyst leaves the reactor, is regenerated in a separate vessel and comes back. Houdry beds were fixed, so each reactor had to stop cracking to regenerate. Lpp. 15–16
    Why did cracking move from the dense bed into the riser?
    Zeolites are much more active than amorphous catalysts (about 100×). With the old dense bed, gasoline over-cracked to gas and coke. A short, controlled riser contact followed by quick separation keeps gasoline yield high. Lpp. 12, 19
    Name the two FCC configurations and one difference.
    Side-by-side: reactor and regenerator are separate adjacent vessels. Stacked (Orthoflow): reactor sits on top of the regenerator. Bpp. 209–210
    What supplies the heat for the endothermic cracking reactions?
    Burning the coke deposited on the catalyst, in the regenerator. The hot regenerated catalyst carries that heat back to the riser. Bp. 199
