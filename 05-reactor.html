    Chapter 05 · reactor

    The riser reactor and regenerator

    Reactor type and why, fluidisation regimes, riser dimensions, heat balance with a worked example, lump kinetics.

    Read: ~6 minSources: B§8.4, 8.8–8.14, Ex. E8.2–E8.4 Lpp. 16–20

    5.1Kind of reactor used

    Exam line

    Vertical riser reactor (dilute-phase pneumatic transport, ~plug flow, 2–10 s) + fluidised-bed regenerator (turbulent); fine zeolite circulates continuously between them. BTable 8.5, p. 210

    • Catalyst deactivates in the riser, is regenerated in the regenerator. Bp. 209 Riser = main reactor (endothermic reactions). Bp. 210
    • Support vessels: disengager + cyclones; baffled steam stripper. Bp. 210
    • Why a riser: zeolites very active → short time prevents over-cracking; separator replaced the pre-1965 dense-bed reactor (see 1.5). Lpp. 16, 19

    5.4Disengager, stripper, regenerator

    • Hydrocyclones in reactor and regenerator catch carried-over solids. Bp. 211 Stripper: steam + baffles strip oil before regeneration. Bp. 210
    • Regenerator: excess air burns coke; air rate sets CO₂/CO ratio or T, kept low enough to protect catalyst. Some units steam-strip regenerated catalyst to remove adsorbed O₂. Lpp. 15–16
    • Flue gas heat: raises steam or drives expanders that compress regeneration air + make power. Lp. 17
    Partial burn (to CO)Complete burn (to CO₂)
    UnitsOlderNewer
    Air~½ of full burn → cheaper blower + running costMore
    Flue gasCO-rich → CO boiler recovers energy—
    CatalystHigher residual carbonMuch lower (range 0.01–0.4 wt%) → more active + selective → better product distribution

    5.5Material and heat balance

    \[ m_f + S_{in} + m_{cat} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} m_i + S_{out} + m_{scat} \;\Rightarrow\; m_f = \sum m_i + m_{coke},\;\; m_{coke} = m_{scat} - m_{cat} \]
    (8.13–8.14) reactor

    Steam passes through (\(S_{in} = S_{out}\)); coke leaves on spent catalyst. Bpp. 216–217

    \[ m_{air} + m_{scat} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} n_i + m_{cat} \]
    (8.15) regenerator

    \(n_i\) = CO₂, CO, H₂O, SO₂, N₂, O₂. Bp. 217

    \[ m_f C_{p,f}(T_f - T_o) + m_f(\Delta H_R) + m_{cat} C_{p,cat}(T_{Reg} - T_R) + S_{in} C_{ps}(T_s - T_R) = (T_R - T_o)\sum m_i C_{p,i} + m_{coke} C_{p,coke}(T_R - T_o) \]
    (8.17) reactor
    \[ m_{air} C_{p,air}(T_{air} - T_o) + m_{coke} C_{p,coke}(T_R - T_o) + q_{coke} = (T_{Reg} - T_o)\sum n_i C_{p,g_i} + m_{cat} C_{p,cat}(T_{Reg} - T_R) \]
    (8.18) regenerator

    \(q_{coke}\) = heat of coke combustion. Bp. 218

    Worked example E8.2: catalyst circulation

    1. Coke (from E8.1) 13,551 kg/h ≈ 1129 kmol/h C → \(q_{coke} = 1129 \times 0.393\times10^{6} = 443.8\times10^{6}\) kJ/h (\(\ce{C + O2 -> CO2}\)). Bp. 218
    2. Air = 1129/0.21 = 5376 kmol/h → N₂ 4247, CO₂ 1129 kmol/h.
    3. T: air 200 °C, spent cat 520 °C, flue gas 700 °C, regen. cat 690 °C, ref. 15 °C. \[ 5376(29.6)(185) + m_{cat}(1.11)(505) + 13551(21.1)(505) + 443.8\times10^{6} = 685\,[1129(46.9) + 4247(32.6)] + m_{cat}(1.11)(675) \]
    4. \(m_{cat} = 2.58\times10^{6}\) kg/h = 43 t/min. Bp. 219 Feed 476,250 lb/h ≈ 216,000 kg/h → C/O ≈ 12 Dfrom E8.1–E8.2, above Table 8.4's 5.4 because E8.1 coke (6.3 wt%) is high: more coke burned → more heat for the catalyst to carry.

    5.6Kinetics: lump models

    Products grouped into lumps. VGO cracking 2nd order, gasoline cracking 1st order, same deactivation \(\phi\) for all. B§8.13

    \[ \frac{dy_1}{dt} = -(k_1 + k_3)\,y_1^2\,\phi, \quad \frac{dy_2}{dt} = \left(k_1 y_1^2 - k_2 y_2\right)\phi, \quad \frac{dy_3}{dt} = \left(k_3 y_1^2 + k_2 y_2\right)\phi \]
    (8.19–8.21) 3-lump

    VGO \(y_1\), gasoline \(y_2\), gas + coke \(y_3\). Bp. 220

    \[ \frac{dy_1}{dt} = -(k_{12} + k_{13} + k_{14})\,y_1^2\,\phi, \quad \frac{dy_2}{dt} = \left[k_{12} y_1^2 - (k_{23} + k_{24})\,y_2\right]\phi \]
    (8.22–8.23) 4-lump

    Gas \(y_3\) and coke \(y_4\) separate. Bpp. 220–221

    \[ C_c = 2.43\times10^{-3}\,t_c^{0.2}, \qquad \phi = \frac{1}{1 + 69.47\,(100\,C_c)^{3.8}} \]
    (8.33–8.34)

    Activity falls as coke \(C_c\) builds with coking time \(t_c\); falls along the riser in E8.4. Bpp. 223, 226

    5.7Quick check

    Regime in riser, regenerator, stripper?
    Pneumatic transport; turbulent; bubbling (also regen → riser standpipe). BTable 8.5
    Why can't coke yield be set independently?
    Coke is the only fuel: its combustion heat must equal reactor heat demand (Eqs 8.17–8.18). Bp. 211
    One advantage of full burn to CO₂?
    Lower residual carbon → more active, selective catalyst → better products. Lp. 17
    C/O in Ex. E8.4?
    144 ÷ 20 = 7.2. Dfrom E8.4.1
