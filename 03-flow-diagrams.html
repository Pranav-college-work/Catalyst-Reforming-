    3.4Process description, step by step

    1. Preheat: feed + recycle heated in exchangers or furnace to 316–427 °C; enters riser bottom with steam. Lp. 16 Bp. 210
    2. Contact: regenerated catalyst (649–760 °C) joins at the riser base; its heat vaporises the feed and brings it to reaction temperature. Bp. 210 Lp. 16
    3. Riser = main reactor: 2–10 s; cracking runs from first contact until vapour and catalyst separate. Bp. 210 Lp. 16
    4. Disengage: vapour → cyclones → fractionator (9); catalyst + some heavy liquid drop into the disengaging zone (mechanical separation). Bp. 210 Lp. 15
    5. Strip: steam + baffles remove oil from catalyst (5). Bp. 210
    6. Spent catalyst (482–538 °C, coke + adsorbed HC) → regenerator (6). Bp. 211 Lp. 17
    7. Regenerate: excess air burns coke (7); air rate controls CO₂/CO ratio or regenerator T; catalyst heats to 620–845 °C, most of that heat returns to the feed. Lpp. 15–17
    8. Flue gas (8): cyclones → power recovery → CO boiler (if burned only to CO) → ESP. Bp. 211 Lpp. 16–17
    9. Fractionate: light gases, heavy gasoline, LCO, HCO, decant slurry; light gases → GCU → fuel gas, propane, butane, LPG, light gasoline (10–17). Bp. 211
    10. Slurry: settled; clarified oil leaves, slurry may recycle to riser with HCO (18–20). Bp. 211
    11. Steady state while regenerator heat produced = reactor heat consumed. Bp. 211

    3.6Quick check

    Trace one catalyst particle around the unit.
    Regenerator (~727 °C) → standpipe + RCSV → riser base, meets feed → up riser 2–10 s, picks up coke → disengager/cyclones → stripper → SCSV → regenerator (coke burned) → repeat.
    Why strip before regeneration?
    Recovers adsorbed HC as product instead of burning it (waste + extra air/heat load). Lp. 17 Bp. 210
    What is in the fractionator bottoms?
    Aromatic slurry with catalyst fines → settled: clarified oil out, slurry optionally recycled with HCO. Bp. 211
