    Fluid Catalytic Cracking

    Heavy gas oil meets hot, acidic zeolite powder for a few seconds in a riser at about 500 °C and breaks into gasoline, LPG olefins and cycle oils. The coke left on the catalyst is burned in a regenerator, and that heat drives the cracking.

    00Unit at a glance

    FCC · unit datasheettypical values · click a chip for the source
    What it is
    Conversion unit: cracks C–C bonds of heavy oil on an acid catalyst Lp. 9 Bp. 199
    Feed
    Gas oil boiling 316–566 °C (AGO, VGO) residue feeds in RFCC after desulphurisation Bpp. 200–201
    Catalyst
    Zeolite Y (faujasite) in a clay matrix ~70–75 µm powder, ~800 m²/g Lp. 16 Bp. 208
    Reactor
    Riser: pneumatic transport, near plug flow plus a fluidised-bed regenerator BTable 8.5
    Reaction temperature
    480–550 °C ≈ 533 °C riser outlet in the max-gasoline case Bp. 208, Table 8.4
    Regenerator temperature
    620–845 °C ≈ 727 °C in the max-gasoline case Lp. 17 BTable 8.4
    Pressure
    1–5 bar reactor ≈ 2.1 barg, regenerator ≈ 2.3 barg Bp. 208, Table 8.4
    Contact time
    2–10 s in the riser lecture quotes 1–4 s Bp. 210 Lp. 13
    Catalyst / oil ratio
    ≈ 5.4 kg/kg max-gasoline case BTable 8.4
    Mechanism
    Carbenium-ion chain, β-scission lecture calls it "carbonium ion" Lpp. 9–12 B§8.5
    Main product
    Gasoline, 35–60 wt% good octane BTable 8.2
    By-products
    Dry gas + H₂S, LPG, LCO, HCO/slurry, coke 3–5 wt% BTable 8.2
