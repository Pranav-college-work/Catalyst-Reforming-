    6.3Conversion and yield correlations

    \[ \text{CONV}\% = \frac{\text{volume of oil feed} - \text{volume of cycle stock}}{\text{volume of oil feed}} \times 100 \]
    (8.12)

    Cycle stock is the part not cracked to gasoline and lighter (LCO + HCO). At 75 % conversion, cycle stock is 25 %. Lecture form: conversion = 100 % − gas-oil yield. Bpp. 212, 214 Lp. 23

    6.5Operating modes

    Max gasoline

    • Feed 446 °F (230 °C), C/O 5.4
    • Riser outlet 991 °F (533 °C)
    • Regenerator 1341 °F (727 °C)
    • Reactor / regenerator 30 / 33 psig
    • Dispersion steam 0.9 wt%

    BTable 8.4

    Max light olefins

    • Severe riser conditions, blended catalyst (Petro-FCC), or new catalysts (DCC, CPP)
    • Propylene 22 wt% vs 4.7 wt% conventional
    • Naphtha drops from 53.5 to 28 wt%
    • ZSM-5 shifts gasoline-range paraffins to C₃/C₄

    B§8.16, Table 8.8, p. 209

    More middle distillate (LCO)

    • Run at lower conversion
    • By Table 8.6, LCO falls as conversion rises in the usual range (e.g. 20.7 LV% at 55 % vs 15.8 LV% at 75 %)
    • LCO must be hydrotreated for diesel

    DTable 8.6 BFig 8.1

    Constraints that limit optimisation

    • Heat balance: steady state only while regenerator heat = reactor heat demand; coke yield is not free. Bp. 211
    • Regenerator burning capacity: high-CCR feed can lay down more coke than can be burned. Bp. 201
    • High coke laydown raises air/oxygen demand, regenerator temperature and the need for heat removal. Lp. 20
    • Regenerator temperature ceiling: overheating deactivates the catalyst. Lp. 16
    • Metals: V destroys activity; Ni makes coke and H₂. Lp. 20

    6.6Conclusion: 8 lines to remember

    1. FCC cracks heavy gas oil (316–566 °C) into gasoline, LPG olefins and cycle oils on a circulating fluidised zeolite catalyst. Bpp. 199–200
    2. Riser reactor: 480–550 °C, 1–5 bar, 2–10 s, pneumatic transport, near plug flow. Bpp. 208, 210
    3. Regenerator burns coke at 620–845 °C; the hot catalyst carries that heat back to the riser. Lp. 17
    4. Catalyst: rare-earth-exchanged zeolite Y in a kaolin/binder matrix, ~70 µm; ZSM-5 for octane and olefins. Lpp. 13, 16 Bp. 209
    5. Mechanism: carbenium-ion chain — protonation, β-scission, rearrangement to 3° ions, hydride transfer. Lpp. 11–12
    6. Net reaction is endothermic; coke yield is set by the heat balance. Bpp. 208, 211
    7. Products: gasoline 35–60, LPG 8–20, LCO 12–20, HCO + slurry 10–15, dry gas 3–5, coke 3–5 wt%. BTable 8.2
    8. Optimise with temperature, C/O, short contact time, catalyst choice, full CO₂ burn and a clean (hydrotreated) feed. Lpp. 17–20 B§8.7

    6.7Quick check

    Define conversion in FCC.
    Percentage of feed volume cracked to gasoline and lighter: (feed − cycle stock) / feed × 100, where cycle stock is the LCO + HCO left in the gas-oil range. BEq. 8.12
    Why can the liquid volume yield exceed 100 %?
    Products are lighter (less dense) than the feed, so the same mass takes up more volume. Lp. 23
    Name three ways to raise gasoline yield.
    Zeolite catalyst; short riser contact with fast separation; optimum riser temperature (not too high); hydrotreated feed; full CO₂ regeneration for a cleaner catalyst. Lpp. 12–20
    You want more propylene. What do you change?
    Add ZSM-5, raise severity, or move to a DCC / Petro-FCC style design. Petro-FCC gives 22 wt% propylene vs 4.7 wt% conventional. B§8.16, Table 8.8
